A small alias table is checked before the fuzzy fallback

- `opportunities.forecast_category` is now generated as the deal's final close-time forecast call rather than as a restatement of the outcome plus independent noise. Previously every won deal was stamped `Commit` unconditionally and every lost deal drew from `rng.choice(["Best Case", "Pipeline", "Omitted"], p=[0.3, 0.4, 0.3])` — a draw connected to no deal characteristic at all, violating the causal-wiring invariant, and a `Commit ⟺ won` mapping that made the field a perfect separator in both directions (Commit 100% won, every other category 0% won). It is now assembled as a perceived win probability on the log-odds scale from drivers already decided at the call site and cut into the same four-value vocabulary at the same conventional pipeline-review bands `fact_forecast_submissions` uses (0.70 Commit / 0.40 Best Case / 0.15 Pipeline). New business is driven by cycle depth within `config.CYCLE_LENGTH_DAYS` (the available progression proxy, since every deal emits the full `config.NEW_BUSINESS_STAGES` list and the stage dates derive from `cycle_days`), loss reason (`price`/`competitive` as a late slip off a confidently-carried deal, `no_decision` as the stalled deal nobody forecast), Enterprise POC outcome, discount position within `config.DISCOUNT_RATE_RANGE`, and rep ramp status as an optimism bias on the call. Renewal/expansion starts from the complement of `config.ANNUAL_CHURN_RATE` and is moved by the contract outcome (expansion / flat / contraction / churned), loss reason, notice-period runway, and the same ramp bias. Win rate by category rank is now monotone and non-degenerate at every cut — overall **Omitted 1.29% (n=465) → Pipeline 5.25% (n=476) → Best Case 27.23% (n=382) → Commit 98.89% (n=8,492)**; Commercial/Enterprise new business alone 0.48% → 1.86% → 15.87% → 81.96%; renewals alone 8.00% → 36.17% → 80.00% → 99.60% — so a Commit slips 94 times and a written-off deal closes anyway 31 times across the window. The ramp bias surfaces as an emergent property: a ramping rep's Commit converts at 87.0% against a ramped rep's 95.9%. SMB new business and migration-driven expansions remain unconditional `Commit` by construction and are now documented as such — a same-day self-serve signup has no forecast cadence to have made a call in, and a migration expansion formalises a committed minimum the account's own usage has already crossed, so neither has a losing branch. The new logic draws exclusively from a dedicated `np.random.Generator` seeded `BATCH2_SEED + 600`, threaded into `generate_new_business_opportunities()` and `generate_renewal_opportunities()` as a separate parameter — the same stream isolation `reassign_orphaned_ownership()` already gets — and the two retired draws are still taken from the shared sequential `rng` and discarded so its position is unchanged. All seven batch-2 outputs were regenerated and diffed column-by-column against the prior files: `forecast_category` in `opportunities.csv` is the only value that moved, and `opportunity_stage_history.csv`, `usage_monthly.csv`, `fact_workflow_chain_events.csv`, `subscriptions.csv`, `mrr_by_account_month.csv` and `committed_vs_utilized_monthly.csv` are byte-identical. Adds two correlational-validity checks in `tests/test_phase1_batch2.py` (monotone, non-degenerate win rate by rank; loss-reason ordering and the ramping-rep Commit conversion gap) and, in `docs/acme-corp-phase1-data-qa-plan.md`, a `Funnel & opportunities` edge case plus the matching correlational-validity test case. `fact_forecast_submissions` is unaffected — it never reads this column, and `test_close_time_only_fields_are_not_read` still passes unchanged
- Forecast's ML lens dropped `days_to_period_end` (days from the evaluation date to its quarter's end) after independent review found it a genuine point-in-time defect rather than a tuning choice: in training every row carries its own evaluation date, so the feature varies and appears informative, but the signal is deal-close-date position within the quarter — a post-close attribute leaking backward. At scoring time every open deal shares one `as_of_date`, so the feature is constant across the whole scored population, contributing zero discrimination while still applying a fixed log-odds offset to every production prediction. Removing it moved the canonical checkpoint's held-out AUC only slightly (0.8098 → 0.8153) and cut the ML lens's aggregate backtest under-forecast bias from −12.13% to −5.29% — direct evidence of a real production-time bias, not just an in-sample artifact. The removal did not explain a separate AUC-ceiling breach at a second checkpoint (0.8613 → 0.8593, still over the 0.85 target); that breach's methods-doc explanation ("holdout composition — a higher renewal share") was independently checked and found factually wrong (renewal share was actually near-identical, marginally lower), and is restated as a genuine, unexplained breach. The leak-ratio diagnostic's own reasoning ("identical ratios at both checkpoints ⇒ composition, not leakage") is corrected as a non-sequitur — the leak-proof baseline is built from inputs disjoint from the model's features, so it cannot detect a leak living inside those features, and a persistent leak would produce the same "identical ratio" signature. A proposed ratio-based AUC ceiling is rejected on the same grounds (it would not have fired at either checkpoint, including the one with a real unexplained anomaly); a composition-standardized within-stratum AUC is added as a diagnostic instead. Several numeric doc inaccuracies are also corrected (a count-weighted vs. dollar-weighted category-close-rate mismatch, a stale quarter count, a discount-rate population mismatch, coefficient/confusion/calibration figures refreshed for the corrected feature set)
- Marketing attribution's `pipeline_generated_formula_is_exact` check was an algebraic tautology — `channel_to_lead_rate` is defined as `leads_created / channel_volume`, so the three-factor product telescopes back to a lead count for any input values, and corrupting `channel_volume` by 1000× still passed. Replaced with `pipeline_generated_rollup_reconciles_to_source`, which independently re-queries `fact_leads`/`fact_campaign_engagement_events` via fresh SQL and checks both that against the rollup's own aggregates and an accounting identity (resolved + open == created) that can genuinely fail. Also corrected a docstring claiming `pipeline_generated` (the created-cohort basis) equals the converted-lead count "by construction" — true only when a period has no open (unresolved) tail, false on 12 of 116 real rows by up to 3,326 leads. The build spec's 0.3% campaign-spend reconciliation figure was being asserted as met at 0.331% (a near miss, not a match) via a tolerance constant mislabeled 0.3% but actually set to 0.5%; now reported honestly as not met, with the 15% level-agreement check (the one that actually gates the artifact) unaffected. The mix-shift TVD floor moves from 0.005 to 0.010 — a 200k-draw null simulation showed 0.005 sits at the 72nd percentile of pure switching noise, not a real discrimination threshold. Several minor doc inaccuracies (a switching-matrix cell, a crossover-count scope mismatch, a code comment's stale conversion count) are also corrected, and the community-gains-under-last-touch finding's generator-routing explanation is restated to lead with the caveat rather than follow it
- `quota_history`: ISR/AE quota is now back-solved from the opportunity generator's realized deal supply — opportunities closed per fully-ramped rep-quarter × that segment's realized win rate × average won deal size — instead of being drawn from ranges sized against `config.ACV_RANGES` with no reference to deal volume. `_QUOTA_BASE_RANGE` moves from AE $400–700K and ISR $150–220K per quarter to **AE $60–105K and ISR $22.5–33K**, each band's midpoint set so the *stated* quota (after the generator's existing ~2%/quarter step-up) lands on the measured anchor of $103,619 per ramped AE rep-quarter and $34,363 per ramped ISR rep-quarter; band half-widths and the step-up mechanic are unchanged. The old ranges implied ~2.6 wins per AE per quarter against an actual 0.35, and were unreachable by construction rather than by performance: total AE new-business pipeline across the whole simulation is 452 opportunities, so even at a 100% win rate the supply covers 54% of stated AE quota. Company-wide attainment moves from **12.9% to 84.4%** across the six-year history (94.8% over the fully-supplied window), and from no rep-year in six years reaching 100% (max 68.5%, median 9.4%) to **36.4% of rep-years at or above 100%**, median 75.7%. `analytics/capacity_planning.py`'s `quota_coverage_ratio` is consequently reclassified from `level_caveated` to `comparable` — coverage runs 0.565–0.969 against a prior 8.5–14.5%, and the achievability verdict is selective (34 of 48 team-quarters over the margin, against 48 of 48) rather than definitional. The level-immune `ramp_attributable_gap_pct` basis is unchanged at 13 of 48, as its level-immunity claim predicts. Adds a cross-generator regression check in `tests/test_phase1_batch2.py`, a `Rep/capacity` edge case and a distributional-realism band in `docs/acme-corp-phase1-data-qa-plan.md`, and restates the Capacity planning entry in `docs/acme-corp-analytics-methods.md` against the recalibrated figures
- Capacity planning's team-quarter aggregation now propagates a not-yet-estimable rep's `NaN` capacity to the whole team-quarter instead of silently dropping it from the sum, which previously fabricated a "zero capacity" reading for teams that simply hadn't accumulated enough baseline history yet in `prior_quarters_only` mode. The decomposition-reconciliation check now runs against both baseline modes, not only the default
- `gtm_plan_targets`: `expansion_consumption_revenue` and `contraction_churned_revenue` are now derived from the benchmark-blended `nrr`/`grr` anchors (`1 − grr**(1/12)`, and `nrr**(1/12) − 1 + that`, re-derived per plan year) instead of being set from standalone monthly shares of the revenue base. The standalone shares compounded to an annual GRR of ≈0.51 and NRR of ≈1.55 against plan rows stating ≈0.91 and ≈1.15, so the plan table stated the same identity two contradicting ways; the two halves now reconcile to machine precision. Adds a table-level regression check in `tests/test_phase1_batch5.py`, and restates the `nrr`/`grr` level-comparability caveat in `analytics/variance_diagnostic.py` and `docs/acme-corp-analytics-methods.md` to locate the remaining gap on the actuals side (gross contraction bucket scope) rather than in the plan generator

All four dashboard pages were rebuilt against this system; see the Fixed entries above for the real bugs that rebuild and its independent audit found
- Phase 5 CRO / leadership interface (`dashboard/`, Streamlit): the digest, forecast, segment-efficiency, and chat views called for in the build spec's Section 6 scope guardrail. Streamlit was chosen over Next.js because every other layer of this system is already Python end-to-end, so the app reads the finished marts and calls `analytics/forecast.py`/`semantic/server.py` in-process, with no separate API layer. The digest view renders `analytics/weekly_readout.py`'s own generated JSON verbatim; the forecast view calls `run_forecast()` live (never logging to `fact_model_performance_history`); the segment-efficiency view reads `mart_growth_bridge`/`mart_efficiency`/`mart_durability`/`mart_segment_migration` directly; the chat view routes a free-text question to `semantic/server.py`'s whitelisted `query_metric()` via deterministic keyword/substring matching against the registry's own names and aliases — never an LLM call, and never a fuzzy substitution past what the registry's own `_resolve_metric()` guardrail would accept. Needs its own Python 3.12 virtualenv (`dashboard/.venv`) for the same reason `semantic/` does — see `dashboard/README.md`
- `lead_scoring_history` (`generators/lead_scoring.py`, `generators/run_batch11.py`): event-grain re-scoring history for every real lead, reusing `compute_fit_score()`'s exact firmographic logic on a ~30-day jittered cadence. A real, dateable model-version change (`lead_fit_v1_preregion` → `lead_fit_v2_region_aware`, at a fixed cutover date) adds the same formula's region term partway through the company's history — not fabricated churn, the identical scoring logic at two real points in its own timeline. The score is stored separately from the eventual outcome and never reads it, by design. Validated by a 28-check suite (`tests/test_phase1_batch11.py`)
- `experiments_registry`/`experiment_assignment` (`generators/experiments.py`, `generators/run_batch12.py`): a formal catalog of this project's one real randomized experiment — the marketing paid/community holdout program already built in Wave 2 — with assignment rows tracing to the real `is_holdout` flag already in the marketing data, and a result block reusing `marketing_attribution.py`'s already-published, already-validated incrementality read rather than a second computation. No new experiment was invented to pad the registry. Validated by a 30-check suite (`tests/test_phase1_batch12.py`)
- Segment/lead scoring model (`analytics/lead_scoring_validation.py`): validates the real, already-generated firmographic lead score against eventual conversion outcomes rather than fitting a new model. Discrimination is modest but real (AUC 0.5420) and close to the score's own construction ceiling; value concentrates almost entirely at the top "Enterprise-only" tier (2.76x lift) with almost none at the broader "Commercial-or-better" cut. Isolates the real v1→v2 region-term model-version change from calendar-time confounding by reconstructing a region-zeroed score on the same rows rather than naively comparing two different time periods, finding a small but statistically real discrimination improvement
- Testing / experimentation methodology & platform (`analytics/experimentation_platform.py`): a reusable, general evaluation framework over the experiments registry — lift measurement with a significance test, guardrail monitoring, sample-size/power context, and registry-completeness governance — built with no experiment-specific logic in its statistical core. Proven correct by independently re-deriving the one real registered experiment's already-published incrementality result from raw marts, through a genuinely separate implementation, and matching it within rounding precision
- Proxy-metric health / analytics investment prioritization (`analytics/proxy_metric_health.py`): a governance catalog, not a time-series drift tool, since this project's own analytics-monitoring history (1-3 checkpoints per model) genuinely can't support one yet — stated as the artifact's headline finding rather than a buried caveat. Catalogs every drift-watch point already flagged across six waves of prior artifacts (26 hooks across 15 models), ranks the metric tree's data gaps by how many downstream nodes each blocks, and surfaces one immediately actionable finding: a gap marked as needing new data collection is already closed by an existing, already-validated artifact, just not yet wired up
- MMM / incrementality-based measurement (`analytics/mmm_incrementality.py`): a log-log media-mix regression of conversions on spend by sub-channel, built to extend incrementality estimation past what `marketing_attribution.py`'s holdout method can reach — organic, which structurally can never run a holdout, and any non-holdout period for paid/community. A full adstock/saturation structure is rejected as unidentifiable at this project's 36-month window (spend autocorrelation 0.78-0.87 makes a carryover parameter statistically indistinguishable from noise); a trend-collinearity check shows a naive model overstates spend's contribution by more than 3x (0.73 vs. 0.22 elasticity) relative to a trend-controlled one. Cross-validated against the existing holdout measurement for the two channels where both exist: the regression materially understates incremental share relative to the holdout, though the holdout's point estimate falls inside the regression's wide confidence interval for both. No channel's effect clears conventional significance at this sample size, and a second checkpoint shows real coefficient instability rather than confirming stability — reported as the artifact's central finding, consistent with a pre-existing, explicitly-flagged tension in the build spec about a thin time series
- Scenario planning / sensitivity analysis (`analytics/scenario_planning.py`): propagates a hypothetical change to any metric-tree Layer-2/3 input through the tree's real, already-validated sum/product/ratio equations up to Layer-1/pillar level, reusing the variance-diagnostic engine's tree structure and baseline computation rather than a second, competing representation. Handles a change that reaches a shared ancestor through multiple paths correctly (a single simultaneous application of the tree's formula, never a sum of separately-computed deltas, which would be wrong for any non-linear node), and reports a scenario touching a `NOT_COMPUTABLE` node honestly rather than fabricating a result
- Retention / expansion cohort analytics (`analytics/retention_cohorts.py`): quarterly acquisition-vintage cohorts tracking logo and revenue retention by months since signup, distinct from segment migration's event lens and the account health score's predictive, account-level one. The headline finding is contract structure surfacing directly in the retention curves with no assumption built in to produce it: SMB (month-to-month, no contract) erodes smoothly from month 1, while Commercial (annual contract) and Enterprise (multi-year) stay flat near 100% through their full contract term and then cliff sharply the month renewal comes due. Long-run retention for all three segments lands inside the project's own benchmark ranges in the expected order; acquisition channel shows no comparable differentiation
- `company_territory`/`rep_territory` (`generators/territories.py`, `generators/run_batch9.py`): a territory dimension sub-dividing the existing `region` field into five territories (NA-East, NA-West, EMEA, APAC, LATAM), assigned to every `market_universe` company and to every account-owning ISR/AE rep (SE and AM roles carry no territory, by design). Rep staffing is deliberately imbalanced against territory company-population share, grounded against real headcount rather than evenly split — APAC under-resourced, LATAM over-resourced against a minimum-viable-staffing floor — giving the territory-coverage artifact a genuine imbalance to detect rather than a null result. Exposed through `dim_reps`, `dim_market_universe`, `dim_accounts`, and `mart_tam_whitespace` (regrained from region×industry×employee-band to territory×industry×employee-band). Validated by a 24-check suite (`tests/test_phase1_batch9.py`)
- Territory / account coverage & routing (`analytics/territory_coverage.py`): a coverage-index diagnostic comparing each territory's share of new-business reps against its share of market opportunity, on three denominators (whitespace companies, high-ICP-fit whitespace companies, existing accounts). Independently confirms the territory dimension's built-in APAC under-coverage and LATAM over-coverage on every denominator tried, more pronounced on some than the design's own stated figure. A structural rebalancing table states what headcount reallocation would equalize coverage, explicitly scoped as a "what it would take" calculation rather than a recommendation — cost, relocation willingness, and ramp time are out of scope
- TAM / ICP / opportunity-sizing model (`analytics/tam_icp_sizing.py`): ICP tiers reusing the real firmographic entry-scoring cut points rather than an invented percentile split, confirming SAM equals TAM at the company-count level (Acme's PLG entry covers the full firmographic space by design) and sizing whitespace opportunity off median realized ACV by segment, with no invented cold-outreach conversion-rate discount applied to the resulting TAM ceiling. Reports an honest negative finding alongside the sizing: ICP fit score alone does not predict later usage-driven segment migration, which is governed by a separate mechanism
- Pricing / packaging analytics (`analytics/pricing_packaging.py`): discount realization by segment, confirmed against the project's stated pricing mechanics; a seeded permutation test finding no relationship between discount depth and win rate or competitive losses, backed by a strong positive control proving the test methodology has real power to detect a relationship when one exists; and packaging/committed-usage calibration against its 65%-of-usage design target, showing commitments converging toward rather than drifting from that target over time
- `fact_sales_activities` (`generators/sales_activities.py`, `generators/run_batch8.py`): event-grain rep-opportunity engagement data (call/email/meeting/demo touches, `competitive_signal`, outbound-touch scoping to Enterprise, meeting booked→resolved chronology), scoped to Commercial/Enterprise new-business opportunities. Engagement volume and quality are real functions of deal outcome, rep ramp status, and an injected underperforming-rep cohort — never independently random — and `competitive_signal` correlates with `loss_reason == 'competitive'` without being a perfect predictor. Implements two incidents the QA plan has required since it was first written but couldn't be built until this data existed: a meetings-rise-without-SQO-rise decoupling period, and a rep cohort visible only in meeting-held rate, independent of ramp status. Validated by a 35-check suite (`tests/test_phase1_batch8.py`) and exposed through `dbt/models/marts/facts/fact_sales_activities.sql`
- Deal-level diagnostics (`analytics/deal_diagnostics.py`): per-opportunity risk detection on open Commercial/Enterprise pipeline, distinct from the variance-diagnostic engine's portfolio-aggregate view. Six named deterministic risk flags (gone dark, single-threaded, competitive pressure, stalled in stage, forecast downgraded, POC at risk) plus a fitted logistic-regression composite risk score, with the full classification validation package (coefficients, out-of-time AUC, confusion matrix at a quantile operating threshold, calibration note). Leakage risk from the generator's reverse-conditioning of engagement quality on deal outcome is checked empirically rather than assumed away: an engagement-only feature set scores AUC ~0.64–0.75 depending on deal-lifecycle position, nowhere near the ~0.93–0.99 range the account health score's calibration note already documents as the leak signature
- Rep productivity & coaching diagnostics (`analytics/rep_productivity.py`): decomposes *why* a rep under-performs into coaching-actionable components — volume-constrained, engagement-quality-constrained, both, or explained by still being in ramp — via a composition-adjusted peer benchmark stratified by segment, deal outcome, and ramp status, rather than comparing the whole team blended together. Structural/descriptive by design: the generator wires no per-rep skill into outcomes beyond ramp status, so the diagnostic's flags correctly show no relationship to attainment or win rate in this data, stated as an expected, checked null result rather than glossed over
- Automated playbook triggers (`analytics/playbook_triggers.py`): three configurable, stored threshold rules (workflow-chain ingestion stall, POC pass rate, post-close committed-Action under-utilization) over binary account/deal conditions, each threshold grounded against the real data's distribution rather than picked. Fills the weekly executive readout's previously-empty "whatever fired this week" section
