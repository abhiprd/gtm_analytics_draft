# Acme Corp — Analytics methods

The Phase 4 equivalent of `acme-corp-gtm-metric-tree.md`: the source of truth for how each analytics artifact is built, what it's validated against, and when its performance has degraded enough to warrant review. Filled in as each artifact is actually built, per the build spec's priority order — not written speculatively ahead of the work.

An entry with no methodology yet is marked TBD, not omitted — the artifact's existence and its place in the priority order are already fixed by the build spec even before its method is chosen.

---

## Point-in-time discipline (applies to every entry below)

Every model in this doc must be computable as of any historical date using only data that existed up to that date — no leakage from the future. This is what makes backtesting and drift detection possible at all; see `analytics-engineering-conventions` for the coding rule this implies.

Historical performance is stored as a monthly time series, not sparse hand-picked checkpoints — `fact_model_performance_history` (model_name, as_of_date, metric_name, metric_value), an append-only log rather than a cross-cutting business mart. Any specific comparison ("today vs. a year ago") is a query against this series, not a separately-run one-off computation.

## Proxy-metric decoupling — general rule, applies to any Layer-3-to-Layer-2 relationship in the tree

A leaf is decoupling from its parent when the trailing 6-month correlation between them drops more than 30% relative to its trailing 12-month baseline, **while the leaf's own raw value has not declined**. That second condition is what distinguishes real decoupling (more meetings, not more wins) from a leaf simply falling off on its own (fewer meetings, naturally fewer wins) — the latter isn't decoupling, it's just decline, and the existing variance-diagnostic engine already handles it.

## Drift review trigger — general rule

Flag for review on **2 consecutive monthly checkpoints** below a stated threshold, never a single reading. One bad month is noise; two is a pattern. This applies uniformly — a specific entry below states its threshold, not a different consecutive-breach rule.

## Account health score / churn-risk model

- **Built**: `dbt/models/marts/marts/mart_account_health.sql` (grain: one row per account_id per month; joins `fact_usage_monthly`, `fact_support_tickets`, `fact_product_logins`, `fact_am_activity`, `fact_subscriptions`, `dim_accounts` so the model reads a single mart) + `analytics/health_score.py` (logistic regression, scikit-learn, `class_weight='balanced'`, seeded via `_RANDOM_SEED = 42`).
- **Model choice and why**: logistic regression, not a more complex classifier (random forest, gradient boosting, a neural network) — three reasons, all tied to this artifact's actual requirements rather than a default pick. (1) Every coefficient is directly attributable to one input feature, which is what makes the relative-importance breakdown and full coefficient table below possible without a secondary explainability technique (SHAP, permutation importance) standing between the model and its own explanation — this artifact needs to be independently auditable, not just accurate. (2) At this dataset's scale (~7,700 accounts, 15 model inputs after encoding), a linear model is a well-justified baseline; a higher-capacity model risks fitting noise rather than signal without a proportionally larger or richer dataset to justify the added complexity. (3) The score's actual downstream use is a ranking (quantile-based risk tiers, not a raw probability read literally — see the calibration note below), and a linear model's ranking behavior is more predictable and easier to sanity-check (e.g., confirming more support tickets always pushes risk in one direction) than an ensemble's. Achieving the QA plan's 0.65–0.80 AUC target with this simpler model is itself evidence the added complexity of a more powerful model class isn't needed to hit the stated bar — not a compromise made to keep things simple.
- **Inputs**: usage trend (account-relative baseline — trailing-3-month mean actions_consumed ÷ the account's own cumulative-to-date mean, not a flat trailing-month comparison), support ticket volume/severity (trailing 3 months), engagement/login frequency (reweighted per the QA plan — multiplied by 1.0 within an account's first 90 days of tenure, by 0.33 after, before being fed to the model as `login_frequency_weighted`), AM sentiment notes (trailing 3-month average `sentiment_score`, null for SMB — no-touch, no AM).
- **Why these four inputs**: usage trend, support ticket volume/severity, engagement/login frequency, and AM sentiment are the account health score's inputs by design, not by feature selection — `docs/acme-corp-gtm-metric-tree.md`'s Account health score leaf and `docs/acme-corp-gtm-portfolio-build-spec.md`'s retention/expansion mechanics both fix these four as the score's inputs at company-model-design time, before this model existed. `analytics/health_score.py` decides how much relative weight each of the four carries in the fitted decision boundary (see Relative importance in the Statistical validation package below); it does not select which signals to include from a wider candidate pool.
- **Label / point-in-time design**: one scored snapshot per account. Churners are scored `_SCORING_LEAD_MONTHS = 5` months before their actual churn month (label=1); survivors get a seeded-random scoring month drawn from their own history, at least 5 months before `as_of_date` (label=0) — a fixed "recent" snapshot for every survivor was tried first and rejected: it clustered every negative example on one calendar month, which degenerated any date-based train/test split. Held out via a class-stratified random split (`sklearn.model_selection.train_test_split`, seeded), not a temporal split — churn events concentrate earlier in the 36-month window (recent-cohort accounts haven't reached their churn point yet, ordinary right-censoring), so a late-date holdout ends up with only a handful of positives and an unstable AUC estimate. Point-in-time discipline holds regardless of split strategy: every row's features are computed only from that account's own data up to its own scoring_date, so shuffling rows across train/test cannot leak future information into any feature.
- **Target**: 0.65–0.80 AUC predicting eventual churn (QA plan grounding target). **Achieved: 0.7613 holdout AUC** (as_of_date 2025-12-31, n_train=5,277, n_test=1,760), logged to `fact_model_performance_history` via `analytics/model_performance.py`.
- **Calibration note (load-bearing, not incidental)**: `generators/config.py`'s `DECLINE_MONTHS_BEFORE_CHURN = 4` is how many months before churn the generator's deterministic usage/ticket/sentiment decline window starts. Scoring at or inside that boundary (`_SCORING_LEAD_MONTHS <= 4`) lets the model see the designed collapse directly and produced an inflated, unrealistic 0.93–0.99 AUC during build — pattern-matching the generator's own construction, not a genuine early-warning signal. `_SCORING_LEAD_MONTHS = 5` (one month before the decline window starts) is what actually exercises an early-warning problem and is what the 0.65–0.80 target describes.
- **Drift threshold**: AUC below 0.65 for 2 consecutive monthly checkpoints → flagged for review
- **Test E validation (QA plan)**: "High-automation, low-login, high-Actions accounts exist and are NOT predominantly mis-flagged as at-risk" — confirmed. Cohort defined per-segment as top-quartile trailing Actions AND bottom-quartile trailing logins (557 accounts as of 2025-12-31). `risk_tier` is quantile-based over the scored active population (top 20% = High) rather than a fixed probability cutoff — `class_weight='balanced'` shifts predicted probabilities up systematically, so a fixed 0.5 cutoff mis-flagged ~72–91% of accounts as High regardless of cohort. Under quantile tiering: population High-risk rate 20.0%, automated-but-healthy cohort High-risk rate 19.7% — at parity with, not elevated above, the general population.
- **Statistical validation package** (as_of_date 2025-12-31, computed on the same 1,760-row held-out split the holdout AUC above is computed on):
  - **Relative importance**: each of the twelve numeric model features is mapped to its one conceptual input (`_CONCEPTUAL_INPUT_GROUPS` in `analytics/health_score.py`), coefficient magnitudes are summed within each of the four groups, and the four group sums are normalized to 100%. `tenure_days`, `is_within_first_90_days`, and one-hot `segment` are excluded from this breakdown entirely (not folded into any group, not given a fifth bucket): they are real model features but not one of the four conceptual inputs this breakdown answers for, and `segment`'s dummy encoding is not on the same scale as the standardized numeric features, so including it would not be a like-for-like comparison. **Result: AM sentiment 34.7%, usage trend 32.0%, ticket volume/severity 27.2%, login frequency 6.0%.** Two caveats travel with this figure: (1) the AM sentiment group's weight is carried almost entirely by whether an account has any AM activity at all in the trailing window (`has_am_activity_3mo`, coefficient −0.322), not by the sentiment score's own polarity (`am_sentiment_avg_3mo`'s coefficient is −0.001, essentially zero) — this partly reflects which accounts have AM coverage at all (SMB has none) rather than sentiment content specifically. (2) `login_count_avg_3mo` and `login_frequency_weighted` are two representations of one underlying login signal (the second is a tenure-reweighted transform of the first), so the login-frequency group's percentage reflects the model's combined reliance on login behavior rather than two independently-contributing signals. Coefficient magnitude on jointly-standardized features is a defensible relative-importance heuristic for a linear model, reflecting the model's own reliance on a signal within its fitted decision boundary — not an independently-estimated, real-world causal effect size.
  - **Coefficient table** (standardized scale — `StandardScaler` applied to all 12 numeric inputs before fitting, so these are relative-magnitude comparisons, not raw-unit effects; sorted by absolute magnitude):

    | Feature | Coefficient |

  - **Confusion matrix, High-risk tier, at the production operating threshold** (top 20% of scored probability = High — same `high_risk_quantile=0.80` `score_accounts()` uses, cutoff probability 0.6678 on this held-out split): precision 0.4261, recall 0.4132, F1 0.4196, from TP=150, FP=202, TN=1195, FN=213.
  - **Calibration note**: mean predicted probability on the held-out set is 0.4431 against an actual base rate of 0.2063 — a calibration gap of +0.2369. Raw `churn_probability` is **not calibrated** to true likelihood (expected: `class_weight='balanced'` shifts probabilities up systematically). `churn_probability` is a valid ranking signal, which is what the quantile-based `risk_tier` relies on; it is not a calibrated probability estimate and must not be read as one.
  - **Sample sizes**: n_train=5,277 (positive=1,088), n_test=1,760 (positive=363) — same split the holdout AUC is computed on.
  - **No R²/RMSE section** — this is a classification model (`LogisticRegression`), not a regression model; deliberate, not an omission.
- **Known limitation**: not yet validated against the QA plan's injected incidents specifically — this build logged one build-time holdout checkpoint only (single as_of_date). Confirming the model's AUC visibly dips at each injected-incident month (and wiring up the recurring monthly backtest the drift threshold above depends on) is drift-monitor's job, still open.

## Segment/segmentation migration analysis

- **Built**: `dbt/models/marts/marts/mart_segment_migration.sql` (grain: one row per migration event -- excludes each account's initial segment-history row -- joined against `int_account_segment_spans` for `days_in_prior_segment` and `int_revenue_movements` for `mrr_reclassified`) + `analytics/segment_migration.py`, which reads `mart_segment_migration` for event detail, `mart_durability` for segment population denominators (migration-rate calculations), and `mart_growth_bridge` for an independent cross-check on graduated revenue (not a competing computation -- both marts derive from the same `int_revenue_movements` rows upstream).
- **Shape decision -- structural/descriptive, not predictive**: this artifact computes migration velocity/rate by segment pair and `trigger_reason`, confirms `firmographic_rescore` migrations actually fire (not just `usage_threshold`), time-in-prior-segment distributions, and "graduated revenue" (MRR reclassified out of the source segment via migration, per the build spec's exclusion of migration from source-segment churn/contraction). None of that is a prediction task -- it is a direct, deterministic aggregation of `mart_segment_migration`'s already-computed event rows. Per `analytics-engineering-conventions`' "Structural/logic artifacts" category (named there for the variance-diagnostic engine, applied here on the same reasoning): no coefficient table, no AUC, no confusion matrix, no R²/RMSE -- forcing any of those onto a population-level descriptive summary would be theater, not rigor. This is stated explicitly rather than left as TBD fields.
- **Model type selection and rationale (why not predictive)**: a migration-propensity/readiness classifier (analogous in shape to the account health score's churn-risk model) was considered and rejected as a genuine fit for this artifact. SMB->Commercial and Commercial->Enterprise migration in this data is generated from a known, largely deterministic rule -- usage/spend crossing a threshold for 2 consecutive months, with noise -- plus an exogenous firmographic re-score path. A classifier trained on usage trend to "predict" migration would mostly be re-deriving the generator's own threshold rule, the same trivial-accuracy failure mode the health score's calibration note explicitly documents and guards against for churn scoring (scoring too close to a deterministic decline window inflates AUC to ~0.93-0.99, which measures pattern-matching the generator's construction, not a genuine early-warning signal). A real predictive segmentation/scoring artifact already has its own place in the portfolio -- build spec item #11, "Lead/segmentation scoring -- model validation & drift detection" (Wave 7, its own methods-doc entry below, still TBD) -- and is not duplicated here.
- **Inputs**: `mart_segment_migration` (`account_id`, `from_segment`, `to_segment`, `migration_date`, `trigger_reason`, `days_in_prior_segment`, `mrr_reclassified`); `mart_durability` (`segment`, `month`, `starting_accounts`) as the population denominator for migration rate; `mart_growth_bridge` (`segment`, `month`, `migration_in_mrr`, `migration_out_mrr`) for reconciliation only.
- **Point-in-time design**: every function takes `as_of_date` and filters to `migration_date <= as_of_date` (or `month <= as_of_date` for the population/reconciliation marts) -- no leakage from the future. Migration velocity and graduated revenue are computed over a trailing 12-month window ending at `as_of_date` (`_DEFAULT_WINDOW_MONTHS = 12`); trigger-reason mix and time-in-prior-segment distributions are computed all-time-to-date as of `as_of_date` (sample sizes are thin enough per trailing window that an all-time-to-date view is the honest one for a distributional read; both respect the same `as_of_date` cutoff). No stochastic step exists anywhere in this module (no train/test split, no sampling, no simulation), so no random seed applies.
- **Validation in place of a predictive target** (computed `as_of_date` 2025-12-31, full history back to 2020-01):
  - **Growth-bridge reconciliation** (structural correctness invariant, not a statistical estimate): summing `mart_segment_migration.mrr_reclassified` by `to_segment`/month (migration-in) and by `from_segment`/month (migration-out) and comparing against `mart_growth_bridge`'s independently-materialized `migration_in_mrr`/`migration_out_mrr` for the same segment/month. **Max absolute diff: $0.00 (2.9e-11, floating-point noise only)** -- reconciles exactly, as expected since both marts share `int_revenue_movements` as their upstream source. A future diff beyond $0.01 would indicate a real bug in one of the two marts, not model drift.
  - **Trigger-reason diversity existence check** (QA plan requirement: `firmographic_rescore` migrations "must actually fire... not just as a schema value that never occurs"): **firmographic_rescore share -- 30.3% overall (245/809), 28.9% within SMB->Commercial (209/722), 41.4% within Commercial->Enterprise (36/87)**. All three comfortably clear the proposed floor below.
  - **Sample sizes**: 809 migration events total as of 2025-12-31 (722 SMB->Commercial, 87 Commercial->Enterprise); trailing-12-month window has 437 and 65 events respectively -- both large enough for the rate/distribution reads above to be more than single-digit-N noise.
- **Target / floor (CONFIRMED by `analytics-model-validator`)**: no accuracy/error target applies (structural/descriptive, per the shape decision above). Two grounded floors apply in its place, in the same spirit as the health score's stated AUC target but newly derived here since the QA plan gives no number for either:
  1. **Growth-bridge reconciliation tolerance: max abs diff <= $0.01.** Not really "proposed" so much as a hard structural invariant given both marts share their upstream source -- included here because the doc's point-in-time-discipline section asks every entry to state a target explicitly, not because there is genuine uncertainty about what the right number is.
  2. **Firmographic-rescore share floor: >= 10% of migrations, both overall and within each segment pair.** Grounded the same way the QA plan's "non-trivial frequency" language is meant, quantified: 10% is unambiguously non-trivial (far above 0%, i.e., real evidence the trigger path fires under realistic generator conditions) while leaving comfortable headroom below the ~29-41% observed at build time, so ordinary period-to-period variation in future checkpoints won't spuriously trip it. This number was not specified anywhere upstream (build spec, QA plan, metric tree) -- it was this artifact's own proposal at build time. `analytics-model-validator` independently checked it against sampling noise on the thinnest segment-pair sample (Commercial->Enterprise, trailing-12-month n=65: binomial standard error ~6.1pp, so even a 2-sigma bad month sits around ~29%, well clear of 10%) and confirmed it as a defensible floor -- not trivially trippable by ordinary variation, still far enough below the observed range to catch a real regression. May reasonably tighten toward 15-20% once more monthly checkpoints accumulate in `fact_model_performance_history`; no basis to do so yet on this single build-time snapshot.
- **Drift review trigger**: per the doc's general rule, 2 consecutive monthly checkpoints below either floor above -> flagged for review. In practice the reconciliation floor is expected to never breach (structural invariant); the firmographic-rescore share floor is the one worth actually watching in `fact_model_performance_history` (`pct_firmographic_rescore_overall`, `pct_firmographic_rescore_smb_to_commercial`, `pct_firmographic_rescore_commercial_to_enterprise`) as more of the timeline accumulates.
- **AM ownership migration** (build spec: "Segment migration also migrates AM ownership... typically opening a renewal/expansion opportunity"): out of scope for this artifact as built -- the analysis here is revenue/event-pattern-focused (velocity, trigger mix, time-in-segment, graduated revenue). AM-ownership continuity at migration is not currently exposed by any `mart_*` table this module is allowed to read from, so it isn't asserted here one way or the other; confirming it would need a Phase 2 mart change, not a Phase 4 workaround.
- **Persistence**: scalar time-series metrics (migration counts/rates by segment pair, firmographic-rescore share overall and by segment pair, graduated-revenue MRR by segment pair, median days-in-prior-segment by segment pair, reconciliation max-abs-diff) are logged to `fact_model_performance_history` via `analytics/model_performance.py`'s `log_performance()`. The full trigger-reason-mix table, time-in-prior-segment distribution table (mean/median/p25/p75/min/max), and graduated-revenue-by-trigger-reason breakdown are NOT logged there (variable-width tables don't fit that log's flat scalar grain, per `analytics-engineering-conventions`' Persistence note) -- they are the structured detail recorded in this entry instead.

## Variance-diagnostic engine

- **Built**: `analytics/variance_diagnostic.py`. Grain: one evaluation month (the last *complete* month at or before `as_of_date` — a mid-month `as_of_date` would otherwise diff a partial month's actual against a whole month's plan). Reads `mart_gtm_plan` (plan side), `mart_growth_bridge` / `mart_efficiency` / `mart_durability` (actuals side, all segment × month, blended here to company-wide grain), and `mart_account_health` (Layer-2 evidence and the watchlist population). No dbt model was added or changed for this artifact.
- **Shape decision — structural/logic, not predictive**: per `analytics-engineering-conventions`' "Structural/logic artifacts" category, which names this artifact specifically: **there is no coefficient table, no AUC, no confusion matrix, no R²/RMSE and no calibration note here, and their absence is deliberate, not pending.** The engine makes no prediction; it walks a fixed tree and compares already-computed mart values. Its correctness claim is "does it identify the true outlier Layer-2 child among true siblings, respecting each branch's real depth," and the only honest way to check that claim is a hand-constructed case with a known unambiguous answer. That check is the synthetic-scenario suite below, run by `analytics-model-validator`.
- **Model type selection and rationale (why not a statistical attribution model)**: a regression-based variance attribution (decomposing a Layer-1 miss onto its children via fitted coefficients) was considered and rejected. The metric tree is already an exact arithmetic identity at every parent — `CLAUDE.md`'s standing invariant is that every parent is the real mathematical result of its children, and `mart_growth_bridge`'s bridge equation is enforced by `tests/assert_revenue_bridge_reconciles.sql`. Where the relationship between parent and children is a known identity, fitting coefficients to estimate it would replace an exact answer with a noisy one, and would introduce a model that could be wrong about arithmetic that cannot be wrong. A deterministic walk of the declared structure is the correct model class here. This is the same reasoning the Segment migration entry above uses for rejecting a migration-propensity classifier.
- **Determinism / seeding**: no stochastic step exists in this module — no sampling, no simulation, no train/test split — so no random seed applies, matching `analytics/segment_migration.py`'s precedent. The synthetic scenarios' "jitter" is a caller-supplied literal sequence, not a random draw, so every scenario is byte-reproducible. The one indirect exception: the watchlist calls `analytics/health_score.py`, which seeds its own model via that module's `_RANDOM_SEED = 42`. This module does not fit, re-fit or replace that model.
- **Point-in-time design**: every loader filters `month <= as_of_date`. `mart_account_health`'s forward-looking columns (`customer_status`, `is_eventually_churned`) are never used to define a historical population — the usage-dip series defines its population as "has a row in this month" and the tenure-at-churn series uses only churns already observed at the month being evaluated.

### Resolved scope decision — playbook triggers are out of scope for this artifact

Build spec Section 5's Phase 4 prose lists "Playbook triggers: binary threshold rules… with a log of which triggers fired" inside the variance-diagnostic engine's deliverable description. Section 8's build-priority order — the section designated as the sequencing authority ("Not an arbitrary ranking — grouped into waves by genuine dependency") — places "Automated playbook triggers" in **Wave 4**, after Wave 1, with the stated dependency "needs Wave 1's thresholds validated against real data first." That dependency is only coherent if the triggers are built *after* this engine's thresholds exist and have been validated, not alongside them. Resolved in favour of Section 8: **no playbook-trigger logic is built here**, and nothing in this artifact reads or writes `fact_playbook_triggers` (which does not exist in `data/raw/` — it remains unbuilt Phase 1 scope). Playbook triggers stay a Wave 4 artifact with its own methods-doc entry when built.

### Variance mechanisms — three, not one

Each Layer-1 row carries a `mechanism` column naming which applies, rather than blending them into a single number:

| Mechanism | Applies to | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `plan_diff` | 8 metrics — new_logo_consumption_revenue, expansion_consumption_revenue, contraction_churned_revenue, consumption_payback, onboarding_cs_efficiency, nrr, grr, logo_retention | `(actual − plan) / abs(plan)` for the evaluation month |
| `trailing_baseline` | Activation only | `(actual − mean of prior 3 months) / abs(baseline)` |
| `not_computable` | magic_number, am_efficiency | Plan value reported; actual and variance are `None` |

**Activation's exception** is by design, not a workaround. `mart_gtm_plan` deliberately carries no plan row for Activation (its own header, and `generators/gtm_plan.py`); the design brief's sample readout reports it as "2.1 days vs. 2.4d last month" — a trailing baseline, not a plan figure. The mechanism (trailing baseline) is that precedent; the window length is this artifact's own resolved decision: the mean of the prior **3** months rather than the single prior month, since a one-month comparator is the noisiest possible baseline. The prior month's raw value is reported alongside (`prior_month_value`) so a readout can render the design brief's exact phrasing. **Degenerate in the current data**: blended TTFA is identically 0 in every month of the 36-month window — `fact_usage_monthly` is monthly grain and every account records its first Action in its own signup month (`mart_growth_bridge`'s own Activation comment states this). The mechanism is implemented and exercised, but on this data the baseline is zero and the deviation undefined, so Activation reports `Not computable` rather than a fabricated `On track`. Its Layer-2 child `onboarding_completion_rate` is likewise identically 1.0. A real TTFA signal needs a day-grain first-Action timestamp in Phase 1 plus a mart change — not a Phase 4 workaround.

**magic_number / am_efficiency — variance from plan is structurally not computable.** `mart_efficiency` leaves both NULL on purpose: no rep-cost/comp data exists anywhere in the raw sources, so the denominator of each ratio is undefined (`generators/config.py` records the same gap — "outbound_sdr's channel spend here covers tooling/data enrichment only, NOT rep headcount cost"). `mart_gtm_plan` still carries a plan value for both, which is not a contradiction: a plan is a top-down target and does not need cost data the way a computed ratio does (`generators/gtm_plan.py` assumes `PLAN_AM_FULLY_LOADED_COST_ANNUAL = 180_000` for exactly this reason). The engine reports the plan value, `actual = None`, `variance = None`, `status = "Not computable"`. **An actual is never fabricated to close this gap.** It closes when a comp/cost source exists in Phase 1, not before.

### Blending actuals to `mart_gtm_plan`'s company-wide grain

`mart_gtm_plan` is published blended/company-wide; all three actuals marts are segment × month. Each metric's blend mirrors how `generators/gtm_plan.py` built the corresponding plan anchor rather than being picked ad hoc:

| Metric | Blend |
|---|---|
| new_logo / expansion / contraction+churn | Straight sum across segments. Company-wide segment migration nets to zero by construction (the bridge identity), so the sum is exact. Contraction and churn are summed into one positive magnitude, matching the plan's stored sign convention. |
| Activation (TTFA) | Signup-cohort-size-weighted mean (logo-weighted — TTFA is a per-account average) |
| Consumption payback | Revenue-weighted (`starting_mrr` share) across **Commercial and Enterprise only**, mirroring `gtm_plan._blend(BENCHMARK_CONSUMPTION_PAYBACK_MONTHS, _final_revenue_mix())`, which renormalises over the segments the QA benchmark table covers (SMB is marked n/a) |
| Onboarding/CS efficiency | Aggregate ratio: total AM/CS touchpoints ÷ total automated Actions across all three segments. Matches `gtm_plan._steady_state_touch_per_action()`, which counts SMB's Actions in the denominator with zero touches in the numerator — a real zero, since SMB is no-touch |
| NRR / GRR | Company-wide **monthly** rate is the aggregate dollar ratio (revenue-weighted by construction); the annual-equivalent figure is the **product of the trailing 12 monthly rates**. Compounding, not averaging — a retention rate is multiplicative across periods |
| Logo retention | Identical, on account counts rather than dollars (logo-weighted), mirroring `gtm_plan`'s use of `_final_logo_mix()` for this one metric |
| Win rate / avg initial commitment (Layer 2) | Commercial + Enterprise only. Build spec Section 2: SMB "gets exactly one Opportunity record… immediately Closed Won… SMB has no win-rate concept to true up." Both legs use the same population so the tree's multiplicative identity is not broken by mixing scopes |

**NRR/GRR/logo_retention unit reconciliation**: `mart_gtm_plan`'s values for these three are annual-equivalent decimal rates while `mart_durability` exposes monthly rates — its header explicitly defers this to "a future consumer." Resolved here by compounding the trailing twelve company-wide monthly rates before diffing, as described above. The two sides are never diffed at mismatched units.

### Level-comparability caveats (`plan_comparability` column)

Three Layer-1 metrics are flagged `caveated` — their variance **is** computed, but the levels are not on the same footing and a readout must not present the gap as a business finding:

- **consumption_payback**: the plan anchor is the QA benchmark payback band (Commercial ~14–18mo, Enterprise ~9–13mo), which assumes a fully-loaded CAC. `mart_efficiency`'s actual CAC comes from `fact_marketing_spend` only and systematically excludes sales headcount cost; its denominator is average utilised margin across the whole installed base rather than per *new* account. Both push the computed actual far below the benchmark band — observed plan variance is ≈ −99% in every month, which measures the definitional gap, not performance. The Layer-2 drill-down is unaffected: it ranks each leg against its own trailing baseline, which is immune to a constant level offset.
- **nrr / grr**: the caveat is the **scope of the contraction bucket on the actuals side**, not the plan. `generators/gtm_plan.py` derives its monthly expansion and contraction+churn shares of base *from* the benchmark-blended `nrr`/`grr` anchors — `contraction_share = 1 − grr**(1/12)` (≈ 0.76%/month) and `expansion_share = nrr**(1/12) − 1 + contraction_share` (≈ 1.95%/month), re-derived at each plan year's own rate — so the plan's flow rows and its durability rows are two readings of one identity and reconcile to machine precision. The gap that remains is definitional: over the twelve months to 2025-11 the marts carry gross contraction+churn at ≈ 5.4% of starting revenue per month against gross expansion at ≈ 10.8%, of which outright churn is only ≈ 0.2%. `int_revenue_movements` buckets *any* month-on-month usage decline as contraction, so in a consumption business whose base is planned to grow ≈ 88%/year both gross legs are large and largely offsetting, while a benchmark NRR/GRR band describes durable downsell in a mature base. That single difference drives both signs at once — TTM GRR ≈ 0.51 against a 0.93 plan, TTM NRR ≈ 1.82 against a 1.17 plan. **Logo retention, which has no gross-flow bucket, reconciles to plan within 2%** — the evidence that the churn leg is sound and it is the contraction bucket's *scope* that differs. Closing it means either a durable-vs-transient split in `int_revenue_movements` or a benchmark stated for gross consumption flows; neither is a Phase 4 workaround, and the Layer-2 drill-down is unaffected (it ranks each leg against its own trailing baseline, immune to a level offset).

### Drill-down logic, and the structural guarantee

The most likely failure mode for this artifact is surfacing an intermediate node — Win rate, a Layer-2 child of New logo — and labelling it "Layer 1". That is made impossible structurally rather than by care:

- `_TREE` is the single frozen transcription of `acme-corp-gtm-metric-tree.md`. Every node carries its own `layer` and `parent_key`; `_register()` refuses any node whose layer is not exactly its parent's layer + 1, and a module-level integrity check re-verifies the whole tree at import (11 Layer-1 nodes: 4 Growth / 4 Efficiency / 3 Durability; the branches asserted two-deep vs. three-deep).
- A metric's layer is **never assigned at emit time** — every emitted record reads `layer` off the node definition.
- A scan can only start at `layer1_nodes()` (nodes whose parent is a pillar). There is no code path by which a Layer-2 node enters the scorecard or heads a drill-down.
- `Drilldown.__post_init__` asserts `layer1.layer == 1`, `layer2.layer == 2`, `layer2.parent_key == layer1.key`, and for every leaf `layer == 3` **and** `parent_key == the Layer-2 node's key`. A mislabelled record raises rather than rendering.
- `rank_siblings()` raises on any candidate that is not a registered child of the parent it was asked about, so "the outlier among its siblings" cannot become "the biggest mover anywhere in the tree."
- Layer-3 depth is read from the tree, never assumed, and reported as one of three explicit statuses: `branch_depth_2` (the tree genuinely stops at Layer 2 here — Consumption payback, Onboarding/CS efficiency, Activation, Logo retention, NRR, GRR), `no_computable_layer3` (the tree has a Layer 3 but no mart computes it), `surfaced` (1–2 leaves ranked and returned). **A Layer 3 is never fabricated to force symmetry.**

**Layer-2 ranking key.** Siblings are ranked by deviation from **their own trailing 8-month baseline** — not from the Layer-1 parent's plan, which would just repeat the parent's miss one layer down. Eight periods carries over the design brief's "trailing 8-week average" shape to this engine's monthly grain. Two bases exist, chosen per parent and reported in a `comparison_basis` column:
- `relative_deviation` (default) — % deviation from baseline. Correct wherever siblings carry different units and only a normalised comparison is meaningful. This is the design brief's own read ("24% this week vs. a 31% trailing 8-week average").
- `additive_share` — absolute deviation in the siblings' shared unit. Used for NRR and GRR, whose children are additive components of one identity all expressed as a share of starting revenue. Ranking those by % deviation lets a large relative swing on a near-zero component (churn at 0.1% of revenue) outrank a component that actually moved the parent (contraction at 5%) — i.e. it names the wrong outlier. Validated by the `nrr_additive_share_basis` synthetic scenario.

The tree's two explicitly non-additive nodes — Marketing–sales handoff quality ("a diagnostic overlay on the above three, not a fourth multiplicative factor") and Brand & awareness ("leading indicator, not summed into the pipeline math") — are registered in the tree but never compete in a sibling ranking.

**NRR/GRR grain note**, attached to those drill-downs at runtime: their Layer-1 figure is the trailing-12-month compounded rate (to match the plan's units) while their Layer-2 drivers are read at monthly grain — the grain at which a driver actually moves. A driver can therefore point the opposite way to the annualised parent in a single month; the driver ranking reads as "what moved this month," not as a decomposition of the twelve-month figure.

Phase 4 code reads `mart_*` tables only (`analytics-engineering-conventions`). Against that scope, **14 of the 26 ranking-eligible Layer-2 nodes have a computable actual, and 0 of the tree's Layer-3 leaves do.** Gaps are reported per node with their reason rather than filled with a proxy; every gap note lives on the node itself in `_TREE` and travels into the engine's output.

Two coverage decisions worth stating plainly:

- **`usage_dip_breadth` (Contraction+Churn's one computable Layer-2 child)** — the share of the month's account population whose trailing-3-month mean `actions_consumed` sits below 70% of that account's own cumulative-to-date mean. This operationalises the node's own stated normalisation ("against the account's own historical baseline") from `mart_account_health` alone. It is descriptive — no model is fitted, no probability produced — so it is not a second risk model. Flagged `partial` because it measures the *breadth* of account-relative dips without performing the cyclical-vs-structural classification the node's headline asks for; that needs the node's second leaf (cohort comparison, same account type, same period last cycle), which no mart supports. The 0.70 cut is this artifact's own resolved decision: deep enough to exclude ordinary month-to-month usage noise, shallow enough to catch a fade well before it reaches zero.
- **Account health score is NOT usable as a Layer-2 trailing series.** `analytics/health_score.py`'s `score_accounts()` defines its scored population as accounts whose `customer_status` is `'Active'` — a final-status field. Scored at a historical month, that silently drops every account that has churned since, biasing older months upward and manufacturing a spurious deterioration trend. Rather than publish a biased series, this node is reported as not computable with that reason attached. The health model's **current-state** output is still used, at `as_of_date` only, for the watchlist. Producing an honest historical series needs `score_accounts()` to gate its population on `churn_month > month`, a change to `analytics/health_score.py` outside this artifact's scope.

Because 0 Layer-3 leaves are computable from the marts, every real-data drill-down currently returns either `branch_depth_2` (honest: the branch has no Layer 3) or `no_computable_layer3` (honest: it has one, nothing computes it). The Layer-3 code path is fully exercised by the `new_logo_miss_win_rate_outlier_with_layer3` synthetic scenario.

### Watchlist

Accounts whose composite health score breaches the risk threshold, ranked by ARR at risk (build spec Section 5). Reuses `analytics/health_score.py`'s scored output — `risk_tier == 'High'` and `churn_probability` — directly; **no second risk model is built.** Grain: one row per flagged account.

**ARR caveat, stated rather than buried**: no `mart_*` table exposes ARR at account grain. `fact_revenue_monthly` does, but it is a `fact_*` table outside the mart-only read scope this Phase 4 code operates under. `est_arr_at_risk_usd` is therefore the account's **segment-average** ARR for the evaluation month (`mart_durability` `starting_mrr / starting_accounts × 12`), not its own contracted ARR — a genuinely computed segment figure, named as an estimate. Because this estimate is constant within a segment, sorting by it first degenerates to "order segments by average ARR, then churn probability within segment" — the ranking does what the build spec's "ranked by ARR at risk" literally asks for, but with a real consequence worth naming precisely: **at `as_of_date` 2025-11-30 the top 25 are 100% Commercial.** Enterprise has the highest segment-average ARR (~$470K) but zero accounts in the High risk tier that period (Enterprise churn probabilities sit below the population's 80th percentile), so it never appears; SMB has by far the largest absolute concentration of risk (1,085 High-tier accounts, vs. Commercial's 33) but SMB's much lower segment-average ARR (~$3.3K) means none of it surfaces until `top_n` exceeds Commercial's 33. A reader must not take "Commercial-only" as "SMB and Enterprise are fine" — it reflects this proxy's segment-constant shape and a ranking cutoff, not the real risk distribution. Exposing account-grain ARR in `mart_account_health` (or a new mart) is a Phase 2 change that would make this exact; a stratified or per-segment top-N is a Phase 4-side option that would surface SMB's concentration without waiting on that mart change, and is worth considering before this watchlist is read by anyone who won't have this paragraph in front of them.

### Threshold: ±8% — PROPOSED, not yet confirmed

Adopted from the hand-illustrated sample weekly readout in `acme-corp-claude-design-brief.md`, which used ±8% to decide which Layer-1 nodes earned a drill-down. Build spec Section 7 lists "exact variance threshold for triggering a drill-down (used ±8% in the sample readout — confirm or adjust)" as explicitly still open, so this is carried forward as the documented starting point, not settled — the same treatment the Segment migration entry's 10% firmographic-rescore floor carries. Breach is symmetric (either direction triggers a drill-down), matching what "±8%" says; direction only decides the Ahead/Behind label.

**Build-time evidence on selectivity** (`measure_threshold_selectivity()`, trailing 12 months ending 2025-11, 8 plan-comparable metrics per month):

| Threshold | Mean metrics flagged / month | Share of metric-months flagged |
|---|---|---|
| ±5% | 6.83 of 8 | 85.4% |
| **±8%** | **6.67 of 8** | **83.3%** |
| ±10% | 6.42 of 8 | 80.2% |
| ±15% | 6.00 of 8 | 75.0% |
| ±20% | 5.75 of 8 | 71.9% |
| ±30% | 5.50 of 8 | 68.8% |
| ±50% | 4.08 of 8 | 51.0% |

At this data's monthly grain, ±8% flags around five sixths of all metric-months — far from the sample readout's 3-of-11 drill-down cadence, and the curve is flat enough that no threshold in this range reaches it: even ±50% still flags half the metric-months. Two structural reasons, neither of which argues for silently moving the number: (1) the sample readout is **weekly** and ±8% was calibrated on that illustrative shape, while every actuals mart here is monthly; (2) `generators/gtm_plan.py` is deliberately decoupled from every other generator's realised output — "a plan that read the actuals it's meant to be compared against would make variance from plan circular" — so plan-vs-actual gaps here reflect that deliberate independence as much as they reflect performance. Note also that the `baseline_variance_pct` column (every metric against its own trailing baseline) is available alongside the plan diff and is the more usable signal for the three `caveated` metrics, whose plan-level offsets are definitional.

### Known limitations

- **End-of-window truncation.** The simulation's final month (2025-12) is not a representative evaluation period: every still-active account's last observed month lands in the contraction bucket (contraction+churn spikes to $1.17M against a $300–500K norm), Enterprise marketing spend is absent so blended CAC is incomplete, and Action volume is partial. `run_diagnostic()` returns a `data_window` block that flags this explicitly rather than excluding the month silently; the canonical build-time checkpoint uses `as_of_date = 2025-11-30`. A checkpoint at 2025-12-31 is also logged; both currently show 7 breaching nodes.
- **No Layer-3 evidence is computable from real data today** — all 32 of the tree's Layer-3 leaves depend on sources that are either ungenerated (`content_engagement`, `community_membership`, leads/campaigns, web traffic, rep cost) or exposed only through `fact_*` tables. Closing any of these is a Phase 1 or Phase 2 change.
- **Not subject to drift monitoring.** This artifact has no calibration to drift. `fact_model_performance_history` carries only its structural coverage and correctness scalars (`synthetic_scenarios_passed`/`_total`, `variance_threshold`, `layer1_nodes_*`, `layer2_siblings_*`, `drilldowns_with_genuine_sibling_comparison`) so a future build can be checked against this one. Recurring `drift-monitor` runs apply to the health score, not here.

## Weekly executive readout

- **Built**: `analytics/weekly_readout.py`. Grain: one readout per evaluation period — the last *complete* month at or before `as_of_date`, company-wide/blended, the same evaluation month `analytics/variance_diagnostic.py` defines. Rendered deliverable and structured payload land in `analytics/outputs/weekly_readout_<as_of_date>.md` / `.json` (version-controlled, unlike `data/acme_gtm.duckdb`, so the real-data document is inspectable without rebuilding the pipeline). No dbt model was added or changed for this artifact.
- **What it is**: Wave 1's fifth and final artifact — the build spec's "forcing function that proves the first four work against real generated data." It assembles the already-built artifacts' outputs into the document structure build spec Section 5 specifies (header · executive summary · Layer-1 scorecard · drill-downs · playbook triggers · forecast · watchlist), in the shape of the hand-illustrated sample readout in `acme-corp-claude-design-brief.md`, backed by real data instead of illustration.
- **Inputs — one, deliberately**: `analytics/variance_diagnostic.py`'s `run_diagnostic(as_of_date)` output, in full: `layer1_scorecard` (all 11 Layer-1 nodes), `drilldowns` (one `Drilldown` per breaching node), `watchlist` (which itself consumes `analytics/health_score.py`'s `score_accounts()`), `coverage`, and `data_window`. **This module reads no mart, no `fact_*` table and no database directly** — it holds no DuckDB connection and issues no SQL. Every business figure enters through the artifact that owns it and that has already been validated against its own stated target, so a number in the readout and the same number in its source artifact cannot diverge.
- **It computes nothing, and that is the design**: there is no arithmetic on a metric value anywhere in the module. Formatting a float into `$45.2K`, grouping the eleven nodes into three pillar tables, and selecting which drill-downs exist (by reading the engine's own `breaches_threshold` flag) are presentation and routing; deriving a figure is not done. One consequence worth stating: Onboarding/CS efficiency renders as **touches per automated Action** — the metric tree's own orientation — rather than the Actions-per-touch inversion the illustrative sample readout showed, because inverting it here would be this artifact publishing a number no mart computed.
- **Model type selection and rationale (why there is no model)**: this artifact is pure assembly, so the question is not which model class but whether a model belongs here at all. Two candidates were considered and rejected. (1) A *scoring/prioritisation model* over the scorecard (ranking which of the eleven nodes most deserves leadership attention) would introduce a second, unvalidated opinion competing with the variance engine's own threshold rule — the engine already answers "which nodes earned a drill-down," and Section 5's drill-down section is specified as variable-length-by-breach, not top-N. (2) A *template-based prose generator* for the executive summary would produce text that reads as narrative while carrying none of the causal reasoning the section exists for; it is excluded on the same grounds the narrative itself is deferred (below). What remains is a structural/logic artifact in the sense `analytics-engineering-conventions` names — the same category as the variance-diagnostic engine and the segment-migration analysis — so **no coefficient table, no AUC, no confusion matrix, no calibration note and no R²/RMSE applies here, and their absence is deliberate, not pending.**
- **Point-in-time design**: `as_of_date` is a required parameter and is passed straight through to `run_diagnostic()`, whose loaders all filter `month <= as_of_date`. This module introduces no additional data access, so it introduces no additional leakage surface. The rendered document names both the `as_of_date` and the evaluation month it resolved to.
- **Determinism / seeding**: no stochastic step exists in this module — no sampling, no simulation, no train/test split — so no random seed applies, matching `analytics/segment_migration.py`'s and `analytics/variance_diagnostic.py`'s precedent. The one indirect exception is inherited: the watchlist section renders `analytics/health_score.py`'s scored output, seeded there via that module's `_RANDOM_SEED = 42`.

### Resolved scope decision — the reporting period is monthly, not weekly

The design brief's sample readout is weekly ("August 24 – August 30, 2026"). Every actuals mart in this project is segment × month, and the variance-diagnostic engine evaluates the last complete month at or before `as_of_date` (diffing a partial period's actual against a whole period's plan is a unit mismatch in disguise). A weekly readout over monthly marts would have to either re-window the marts inside this artifact — the one thing a pure-assembly artifact must not do — or repeat a single monthly figure under four weekly labels. **Resolved: the reporting period is the evaluation month**, and the rendered header says so in place of borrowing the sample's weekly framing. The artifact keeps the name "weekly executive readout" (the build spec's and Section 8's own name for it); the cadence question is a grain question about the marts, not a naming one.

This settles the input build spec Section 7's open threshold question was waiting on. The Variance-diagnostic engine entry's selectivity table records that ±8% flags ~83% of metric-months at monthly grain against the sample readout's illustrative 3-of-11 weekly cadence, and notes that resolving Section 7 "is a decision for the readout build, when the reporting period's grain is fixed." The grain is now fixed at monthly. **The threshold itself is not changed here**: it belongs to the variance-diagnostic engine, this artifact consumes it read-only, and ±8% remains PROPOSED per that entry. What this entry adds is the settled premise — any revision to the threshold should be argued at monthly grain, against that selectivity table, and made in the engine.

### Target — PROPOSED, not yet confirmed

No accuracy or error target applies (pure assembly, per the shape decision above). Three structural/trace criteria stand in its place, in the same spirit as the health score's stated AUC band but newly derived here, since nothing upstream states a number for an assembly artifact. All three are stated as **exact equality rather than a tolerance band**, and that difference from the health score's 0.65–0.80 range is the grounding, not an oversight: an AUC band exists because a fitted model's measured performance carries genuine sampling variance, whereas every check below is deterministic and re-derivable from the same inputs with no variance at all. A tolerance on any of them would imply an error source that does not exist, and would quietly license exactly the failure the criterion is meant to catch.

1. **Structural completeness: exactly 11 Layer-1 rows in the scorecard, every period, unconditionally — 11/11, no tolerance.** Build spec Section 5 says "all 11 nodes, every week." Any number other than 11 means a node was dropped or an intermediate node was admitted; both are defects, neither is drift. Includes the two nodes with no computable actual (`magic_number`, `am_efficiency`) and Activation's degenerate status — they are shown with their reason, not hidden.
2. **Drill-down cardinality fidelity: the drill-down count equals the number of scorecard rows with `breaches_threshold == True`, exactly, and the keys match in order.** Equality, not a band: "never padded to a fixed count" (Section 5) and "never truncated" are both violated by any inequality, in either direction. Zero is a valid result and renders as an explicit statement that nothing breached.
3. **Source-trace fidelity: 100% of the readout's numeric fields re-read identically from a freshly-run `run_diagnostic()`, at exact float equality.** Since assembly performs no arithmetic, a non-zero difference anywhere means a computation was introduced into the artifact that is specified not to compute — which would place that computation outside the validation of the artifact that owns it.

`analytics-model-validator` confirms or adjusts these three as the right criteria for an assembly artifact before they are treated as settled — they carry the same PROPOSED status the variance engine's ±8% threshold and the segment-migration analysis's 10% firmographic-rescore floor carry.

### Validation — structural and trace checks, not statistics

### Drift monitoring — not applicable

This artifact has no calibration to drift, on the same reasoning the Variance-diagnostic engine entry gives. Its three criteria above are binary structural invariants with no sampling variance, so the doc's general 2-consecutive-checkpoint rule does not apply: a single failure is a defect to fix, not a trend to watch. Recurring `drift-monitor` runs apply to the health score, not here.

**Persistence**: structural/trace scalars are logged to `fact_model_performance_history` via `analytics/model_performance.py`'s `log_performance()` — `structural_checks_total` / `structural_checks_passed` (20/20), `layer1_nodes_in_scorecard` (11), `layer1_nodes_not_computable` (2), `drilldowns_rendered` (7), `watchlist_accounts` (25), `sections_not_yet_built` (2). The rendered document and the full structured payload do not fit that log's flat scalar grain and live in `analytics/outputs/` instead.

### Known limitations

- **Every gap the readout displays is inherited, not introduced.** The two not-computable Layer-1 nodes, Activation's degenerate TTFA, the three plan-comparability caveats, the absent Layer-3 evidence and the watchlist's segment-average ARR estimate are all the source artifacts' limitations, reproduced verbatim with their own explanations attached. They close where those artifacts close them — in Phase 1 or Phase 2 — not here.
- **The document is long because the caveats are long.** Each caveated metric carries its full explanation inline rather than a truncated version, on the reasoning that a reader deciding whether a −44.7% GRR variance is a business finding needs the whole reason it is not. A narrative step, when built, is the piece that turns this into an executive-length read.
- **No rendered-output retention policy.** One `.md`/`.json` pair per `as_of_date`, overwritten on re-run for the same date. Whether readouts accumulate as a historical series or only the latest is kept remains open, and does not need settling until the artifact runs on a cadence.
