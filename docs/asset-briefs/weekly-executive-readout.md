# Weekly Executive Readout

## What this is

The single document a Chief Revenue Officer and the GTM leadership team would actually sit down and read: one page, per reporting period, that shows how the business is doing against plan across all eleven top-level GTM health measures, explains why any that missed actually missed, and flags which accounts are most at risk of walking away. It's built for GTM leadership as the regular operating-cadence read, not a one-off analysis.

## Why it exists

Building a metric tree, a health score, a migration analysis, and a diagnostic engine only pays off if leadership actually sees the results in one place, on a regular cadence, in a form built to be read rather than queried. This is that artifact — the capstone that proves the rest of the core measurement loop genuinely works together against real data, not just individually. It's the intended final stop for a leader who wants the state of the business without having to open four different tools to get it.

## How it works

Each period, the readout pulls together everything the other three artifacts have already produced — the eleven headline scorecard figures, the causal explanations behind any real miss, the sales forecast, and the list of at-risk accounts — and lays it out in one consistent document: a scorecard up top, a section that digs into whatever actually went wrong that period (with a note on whether the same driver has been the problem for more than one month), the forecast for the quarter in progress, a watchlist of the accounts most worth a leader's attention, and a short view of whether the business is moving upmarket. It doesn't calculate a single new figure itself; every number it shows was already produced and checked by the artifact responsible for it, and the readout's only job is to assemble, format, and present those numbers faithfully, the same way a chief of staff assembles a board packet from other people's already-approved slides rather than redoing the analysis.

## Key decisions and why

- **The readout is built to add nothing of its own to the numbers it shows.** Every figure is read directly from the artifact that owns and has already validated it, rather than recalculated or adjusted along the way — this was independently confirmed by spot-checking several figures end to end and finding them identical, bit for bit, back to their source. A document meant to be trusted at a glance can't be the place where a number quietly drifts from the analysis behind it.
- **The scorecard always shows all eleven top-level measures, every period, with no exceptions** — including the one that behaves in a structurally unusual way in this dataset (Activation) and the five that carry a plan-comparability caveat, the two cost-based efficiency ratios among them. Each of those is shown honestly, with its own gap explained, rather than dropped from the page to make the document look tidier than the business actually is.
- **The section that explains what went wrong is only ever as long as it needs to be.** It shows one entry for every measure that actually missed its target that period and nothing more — never padded out to look thorough, never trimmed to look better, including periods where nothing missed at all and the section says so plainly rather than disappearing.
- **Each explanation says whether the same driver has been the problem before.** A miss that traces to the same cause two months running reads differently from a one-month swing, so every drill-down carries a streak: how many consecutive months that same driver has been the largest adverse outlier against its own recent history, flagged when it reaches two. It counts only months where at least two sibling drivers could be compared, resets when a different driver takes over, and does not count the final, partial month of the data. The streak is computed by the diagnostic engine and only displayed here. It is a repeat detector, not a diagnosis: in this data it fires about as often as it would if the months were shuffled at random, so a flag means "this driver has led the adverse moves two months running", not that the pattern is proven to be non-random or has a single cause. The page prints that caveat under every streak.
- **The upmarket view leads with rates and mix, not counts.** Accounts only ever move up a segment in this business, so the number of graduations and the revenue that moves with them tend to rise as the base grows. A rising upmarket share is also partly reclassification: the revenue of accounts that move up a segment moves with them, and in the twelve months to November the revenue that moved from SMB to Commercial equals about 7% of company revenue, more than the 5.2-point rise, so the share says where revenue is booked more than who the company is selling to. The graduation rate is shown against the segment the accounts left, and the revenue that graduated can look large against SMB (about 60% of its November revenue) because the accounts that move are about six times the size of the average SMB account. The section therefore puts each segment's share of recurring revenue first, with how that share changed against last month and a year ago, and shows graduation as a rate against the segment the accounts left, set beside the same rate a year earlier. It is not shown for the last month of the data, which is partial.
- **The at-risk account list is drawn straight from the account health score rather than built as a second, separate risk judgment.** Maintaining two different opinions about which accounts are risky would leave leadership guessing which one to trust; reusing the same validated source keeps the whole document internally consistent.
- **The plain-language summary at the top is written by an AI model, but nothing it writes is published until it passes a mechanical check.** The model is handed the finished readout, asked for a few short statements that name the specific driver the diagnostic engine ranked first, and required to say which parts of the readout each statement relies on. A separate, non-AI checker then confirms that every figure in the text appears in a part of the readout the statement cites, with the same rounding, the same kind of unit (dollars, percent, multiple, count) and the same sign; that dates, quarters, accounts and sources exist in the readout; that the named driver is the one the engine actually ranked; that segment, forecast-view and metric names sit beside their own figures where that can be told; and that the caveats travel with the numbers they qualify. It also rejects forward-looking promises and causal claims the readout cannot support. The check does not decide whether the summary tells the right story or is a correct explanation of why a number moved. Text that fails is sent back once with the specific problems; text that fails twice is discarded and the page says so. There is no fallback to a canned sentence.
- **Without an AI key, the summary is simply not generated, and the page says so and why.** A templated stand-in would read like an explanation without reasoning through the cause, which would be worse than an honest gap. The forecast section is held to the same standard: when no forecast can be produced for a period, it says so and why, and shows no number.
- **The forecast section shows the forecast artifact's own four views side by side and picks no winner.** The readout reports a month, but the forecast looks at a quarter, so the section carries the weekly forecast call that was current at the end of the reporting month (the latest Friday call on or before that date) and the quarter it sits in, and says how many days that quarter has left. Nothing after the month's end is read. The four views (the rep's own calls, the manager's calls, the machine-learning estimate, and the manager's view adjusted by the CRO's logged override) stay separate, with the forecast artifact's own flag for when they disagree materially. The readout never recalculates them; a fresh run of the forecast is compared against the section figure by figure. No plan or quota comparison is shown, because no plan exists in the same unit and at the same level, and the section says so rather than inventing one.

## What drives the result

- **The Layer-1 scorecard** — all eleven top-level GTM health measures, shown every period without exception
- **The variance-diagnostic engine's drill-downs** — a deeper explanation for each measure that actually missed its target that period, reused exactly as that engine produced it
- **The account health score's watchlist output** — the accounts flagged as most at risk, ranked by how much revenue is estimated to be on the line
- **The segment-mix view** — each segment's share of recurring revenue, graduation rates out of SMB and Commercial, and graduated revenue, read from the segment migration analysis and the revenue bridge
- **The AI-written summary** — a few short statements, checked against the readout before anyone sees them, or an explicit note that none was generated
- **The sales forecast** — the forecast artifact's four views of the quarter in progress for Commercial and Enterprise, for the forecast call that was current at the end of the reporting period, with its accuracy context and caveats, or an explicit note that none could be produced

## Current result

As of the November 2025 reporting period, the readout renders correctly on every check it's held to: all eleven top-level measures present every time, a drill-down section that lines up exactly with the count of measures that actually missed target that period (nine, in this period), and a watchlist pulled straight from the health score's own output. A second period was checked the same way and produced a consistent shape, and a deliberately impossible-to-trigger test period confirmed the drill-down section correctly renders as "nothing missed" rather than an empty gap when that's the honest result. Thirty-three separate checks were run against the document — covering both the underlying data behind it and the actual rendered page — including checks added specifically to make sure a number printed on the page can never quietly drift from the number it was supposed to show, a fresh run of the forecast artifact compared against the forecast section, and fresh runs of the diagnostic engine and the segment analysis compared against the streak notes and the upmarket view. All thirty-three passed. In June, three drill-downs carry a flagged streak (Renewal win rate under Contraction + churned revenue for three months, NRR's expansion share for five, GRR's contraction share for two); in November none does. Commercial and Enterprise together hold 87.9% of recurring revenue in November, 5.2 percentage points more than a year earlier, and SMB accounts graduated to Commercial at 10.7% a year against 6.2% the year before. The November period shows the 28 November forecast call for the fourth quarter, with both segments' views within the forecast artifact's disagreement threshold. The June period shows the 27 June call, three days before its quarter ended: only two Commercial deals were still open, the CRO's override outweighs them, and the artifact's own flag marks the views as diverging materially, so that figure is a late-quarter artifact, not a read of the quarter.

## Known limitations

Every gap this document shows is inherited from the artifacts that feed it, not introduced here, and each carries its own explanation forward rather than being smoothed over. The most consequential one to know about going in concerns the at-risk account watchlist: because there's currently no way to know an individual account's actual contract value — only the average for its segment — ranking accounts by estimated revenue at risk skews the list toward whichever segment has the largest typical contract size. In the current period, that means the watchlist at its default length shows only Commercial accounts. No Enterprise accounts are at risk this period, and SMB — which actually has by far the largest number of at-risk accounts — doesn't appear until the list is made longer, purely as a side effect of how the estimate is built rather than because those accounts matter less. This is a known, explained consequence of a stand-in revenue figure, not a defect, and it resolves once real account-level revenue data exists to rank against.

The forecast section's machine-learning figures vary slightly each time the underlying tables are rebuilt (up to about half a percent on a forecast value and about 0.001 on the model's accuracy score, with every flag and verdict unchanged), because the tables do not fix a row order; the section says so, and a rebuild can mark an already-generated summary stale so it is regenerated. The scorecard header counts a node as not computable when its card says so (one this period: Activation, whose time to first action is zero in every month).

The streak and the upmarket view each have a limit worth knowing. A series that drifts steadily in one direction stays above or below its own recent average for months, so a trend can read as a streak, and branches with only one comparable driver (Expansion, Magic number, AM efficiency) show "not applicable" instead of a streak. A segment's share of revenue moves with expansion, contraction and churn inside each segment as well as with graduation, so a rising upmarket share is not by itself proof that graduations are the cause. The AI-written summary does not draw on the upmarket view, and the summaries generated before these two additions are out of date and are regenerated when a key is available.

Three smaller notes for completeness: the forecast looks only at deals still open at the call, so a call made in a quarter's last days covers very few deals and the CRO's quarter-level override is not scaled down to match; the forecast is in opportunity dollars for Commercial and Enterprise only, so it is not comparable with the scorecard's monthly revenue movements and is not added to them; and the summary at the top has not yet been generated from a live AI key in this build, so the committed readouts show it as not generated; what has been verified is the checker (against the real readouts, including deliberately corrupted summaries it must reject) and the behavior with and without a key. Note also that a summary that passes the check quotes the readout faithfully; it is not proof that the readout's ranking identifies the true cause or that the summary draws the right conclusion: in both checked periods the top driver is a single-candidate read, and a good summary says so; and there is currently no policy for keeping a historical archive of past readouts versus overwriting each period's document with the next — that's an open question that doesn't need settling until the document runs on a regular cadence.
