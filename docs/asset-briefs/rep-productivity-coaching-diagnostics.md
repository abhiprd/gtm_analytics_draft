# Rep Productivity & Coaching Diagnostics

## What this is

A per-rep coaching diagnostic for frontline sales managers that goes one step past "this rep is behind" and answers *why*: is the rep simply not doing enough outreach, doing enough outreach that isn't landing, both, or does what looks like a shortfall actually disappear once the rep's tenure on the job is fairly accounted for — in which case it isn't a coaching problem at all. It's built for sales managers planning 1:1s and coaching priorities, not for the aggregate, team-level questions capacity planning and the weekly leadership readout already answer.

## Why it exists

Capacity planning already tells leadership whether a team's target is realistic given its roster mix, and the weekly readout already surfaces when a top-line number is off plan. Neither of those tools names an individual rep or says what a manager should actually do about it in a 1:1. This diagnostic closes that gap: it takes the same kind of activity data a manager already has partial visibility into — call volume, meeting attendance, email response, how many people at an account a rep is actually engaging — and turns it into one clear, named verdict per rep that points a manager toward a specific coaching conversation instead of a vague sense that a number is low.

## How it works

For every rep who owns new-business deals and carries a real sales target, the tool looks at the past year of that rep's activity: how much outreach they're doing per deal, how often their meetings actually happen versus fall through, how often their calls connect, how often their emails get a reply, and how many different people at each account they're engaging rather than relying on a single contact. Raw numbers alone would be unfair to compare rep to rep, because a rep working mostly lost deals, a different segment, or still new on the job naturally looks different from a fully up-to-speed rep working a stronger book — none of that reflects real skill. So each rep is compared only against other reps in a genuinely similar situation: the same segment, the same deal outcome (won or lost), and the same stage of ramping up. That fair comparison produces an expected level for each rep, and a rep is only flagged when their actual numbers fall meaningfully short of it. A rep who falls short on outreach volume specifically is flagged as volume-constrained; a rep whose volume is fine but whose outreach isn't landing is flagged as quality-constrained; a rep short on both gets both; and a rep whose numbers only look low because they're still ramping — and who clears the bar once fairly compared to other ramping reps — is explicitly cleared and labeled "ramp-explained," not flagged at all, so a manager doesn't waste a coaching conversation on tenure. A rep with too little activity in the window to say anything reliable is reported as such rather than force-fit into one of the other verdicts.

## Key decisions and why

- **A rep is compared against a genuinely similar peer group, not the whole team blended together.** Comparing every rep against one company-wide average would make a rep working mostly lost deals, a tougher segment, or still ramping look artificially worse for reasons that have nothing to do with skill. Building the comparison group from reps in the same segment, the same deal outcome, and the same ramp stage — and removing the rep's own numbers from that group's benchmark before comparing — keeps the comparison fair and keeps a rep's own results from skewing the bar they're measured against.
- **A direct, transparent comparison was used instead of a predictive statistical model.** Activity records already reflect whether a deal closed by the time they're logged — a rep naturally looks like they engaged more effectively on a deal that won than one that lost, regardless of actual skill, because closing a deal changes how the rest of the engagement on it gets logged. A model trained to predict outcomes from that same activity data would mostly be reading back information it already had rather than finding a genuine coaching signal, so a transparent, rule-based comparison was used instead — every flag can be traced directly to a specific number and a specific fair comparison, with nothing hidden inside a fitted model.
- **Which reps and which deals are covered is fixed by what the business actually tracks a quota against, not chosen by the model.** Only Inside Sales reps and Account Executives working new-business deals carry a real dollar target in this business, so this is the population covered — the same population capacity planning already scores, kept consistent across both tools rather than defined separately.
- **Outreach volume and outreach pace are treated as one signal, not two.** Because touching a deal more often and touching it at a tighter cadence are two views of the same underlying pattern in how reps work a deal, counting both separately would double-count the same signal and overstate its weight relative to genuinely independent measures like meeting-held rate or reply rate.
- **A rep is only flagged if they fall in the bottom one-fifth of their fair peer group, not against a fixed cutoff.** Real performance gaps don't necessarily follow a predictable, bell-shaped pattern, so ranking within the peer group is a more robust way to catch a genuine minority of underperformance than a fixed numeric line would be.
- **A metric only counts once there's enough real activity behind it.** A rate built from a handful of meetings or calls is noise reported with false confidence, not a real signal — a rep's numbers on a given dimension are only used once there's a meaningful amount of real activity to compute them from, and a comparison group is only used for flagging once it has enough reps in it to make a bottom-one-fifth cut meaningful in the first place.
- **A rep can never be labeled "ramp-explained" while also carrying a genuine flag.** Ramp status is already factored into the comparison itself — a ramping rep is compared against other ramping reps, not against the fully-ramped population — so a gap that survives that adjustment is a real gap, checked directly and never allowed to be waved off as "still new" once it's already been fairly adjusted for.
- **Each rep's quota attainment and win rate are reported alongside the diagnostic for context, never as an input into building it.** Feeding attainment or win rate into the flag itself would reintroduce the same circularity the tool is built to avoid — using an outcome to diagnose an outcome — so those figures are shown only as background.

## What drives the result

- **Four outreach signals per rep**: how much outreach happens per deal, how often scheduled meetings actually happen, how often calls connect, how often emails get a reply, plus how many different people at an account a rep engages rather than working through a single contact
- **A trailing one-year window** of activity, refreshed at each check-in, long enough to give every active rep a reliable read without turning into a career-long average
- **The fair-comparison groups**: segment, deal outcome (won or lost), and ramp stage — the three things a rep's raw numbers are adjusted for before any flag is assigned
- **Ramp stage**: a rep is considered still ramping for roughly their first six months on the job, matching how the business already treats new reps in capacity planning
- **Reliability minimums**: a rep's numbers on a given outreach signal only count once there's enough real activity behind them — at least 8 resolved meetings, 10 connected calls, 10 sent emails, or 3 closed deals, depending on the metric — and a comparison group needs at least 10 reps in it before a bottom-one-fifth cut is applied at all
- **The flagging bar**: a rep lands in the bottom one-fifth of their fair peer group on a given signal
- **The ramp-explained threshold**: a rep is only cleared as ramp-explained (rather than genuinely flagged) if at least half of their deals in the window were opened while they were still ramping, and their adjusted numbers clear the bar for their peer group

## Current result

As of December 31, 2025, 40 of the 44 reps who carry a real sales target have enough new-business activity in the trailing year to be diagnosed; the remaining 4 closed no qualifying new-business deals in the window and are reported as having no data to diagnose, rather than being scored as fine by default.

The tool's own checks confirm it's doing real, non-trivial work rather than just re-stating raw rankings: every one of five hand-built test cases with a known correct answer — a rep short on volume only, short on quality only, short on both, genuinely on par, and a rep whose raw numbers look low purely because of ramping — resolved to the correct verdict. Checked against real data at two points in time (December 2025 and June 2025), the fair-comparison version of the quality flag substantially agrees with a simple raw ranking on who looks worst (87.5% and 75% overlap respectively) while still meaningfully disagreeing on some reps — which is the point of the fair comparison, not a flaw in it. One real rep from this build illustrates why: a rep whose raw meeting-held rate put them in the bottom fifth of the whole team turned out to have spent the entire window ramping up; once fairly compared against other ramping reps specifically, that same rep's engagement quality cleared the bar comfortably and was correctly not flagged — while a separate, genuine gap in that same rep's outreach volume survived the fair comparison and was correctly flagged, since a volume gap that persists even against other ramping reps isn't explained by tenure.

## Known limitations

- **This diagnostic's flags do not currently show a measurable relationship to quota attainment or win rate in this dataset, and that is expected rather than a weakness of the method.** This simulated business doesn't build real, individual rep-skill differences into which deals a rep is assigned or wins — beyond overall ramp status, which deal to which rep is effectively a coin flip in how this data was generated. A diagnostic built to find a real skill signal correctly finds none where none was built into the data; a strong correlation here would actually be more suspicious than reassuring. What this means practically: this tool's coaching value can be demonstrated in its logic and its checks, but not yet proven against real business outcomes in this dataset — that would need either a live deployment or a future version of the underlying data that wires in genuine, rep-level skill variance.
- **The bar that confirms this diagnostic is finding a real, non-trivial signal (rather than just noise) is a first-pass judgment call**, grounded in this build's own two check-ins rather than a longer track record. It's treated as a starting point to revisit as more history accumulates, not a settled number.
- **The "still ramping" share of a rep's book is counted by number of deals, not by how much outreach went into each one.** A rep with one large, heavily-worked ramped deal and two small, lightly-worked ramping ones would be read as "predominantly ramping" by deal count even though most of their actual effort happened after they were fully up to speed. This is a real simplification worth knowing about, not an inconsistency — it matches how this tool (and the business generally) already counts a rep's book everywhere else.
- **A rep with zero qualifying new-business deals in the trailing year — for example, someone who spent the window entirely on renewals, or whose book was reassigned after leaving — simply has no data to diagnose for that window.** Widening the window would recover some of these reps at the cost of how current the read stays.
- **Every rep's book is treated evenly regardless of deal size.** A rep working a handful of large deals and a rep working many smaller ones get the same treatment in how their flag is built, even though the segment-based comparison already accounts for some of that difference.
- **There's no way to connect a coaching flag to a dollar cost of underperformance**, since no compensation or rep-cost data exists anywhere in the underlying system — the same gap capacity planning's cost-related figures already run into.
